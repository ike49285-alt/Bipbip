The finding, and its null

The last candidate sits inside the noise it was tested against

A barrier-labelled gradient-boosted model on a 20-ETF levered panel — 635,320 bars, 45,647 held-out trades — beat always-long by +1.25 bps. Against 100 label shuffles of the identical procedure, that value does not reach the null's own 90th percentile. Fifteen shuffles carrying no information at all beat it outright.

p = 0.158 Permutation p-value, Phipson–Smyth. The prior here is that the default hypothesis is zero, and this does not clear it.

An earlier run reported p = 0.069, and that number was itself wrong. Its permutation looked up (symbol, permuted timestamp) pairs and silently fell back to the row's own label when the lookup missed — so 23.4% of every "null" run was real signal. That inflates a null and biases it against the finding.

Fixing it still made the finding look worse, which is worth understanding rather than shrugging at. The null's centre fell as it had to, from +0.33 to +0.11. But restricting to the common grid moved the train/test cut later and cut the sample from 66,256 trades to 45,647, so the real margin fell from +1.63 to +1.25; and the smaller sample widened the null from sd 0.83 to 1.02. The centre moved 0.22 toward the finding; the spread and the margin moved 0.57 against it.

One confound is unresolved and stated deliberately. The correction changed two things at once — null method and sample — and these runs cannot separate them, because on the common grid the old fallback never fires and the two methods are identical there. Both 0.069 and 0.158 sit above 0.05, so the verdict does not turn on it.

The durable output

How this project fooled itself

Each of these produced a false positive here, and each was found only because something else was audited. They are cheap to check and expensive to miss. This is the part worth carrying to any other project.

A silent fallback is how a null gets contaminated

A failed index lookup defaulted to the row's own label instead of raising. Assert that a shuffle actually shuffled: measure the share of rows still carrying their original label.

23.4% of every "null" run was real signal

Five nulls is not a null

Significance is decided by the null's spread, and five draws cannot measure it. Worse, five draws return "beats every null" most of the time on data that is not significant — the control reads as confirmation.

sd 0.415 estimated against a true 0.829 · false "survives" 72% of the time

Check that the event fires on what you think

A round-number condition compared a distance capped at 0.50 against a threshold of 0.0015 × close, so above $333 it matched every bar. It selected expensive stocks, not round prices, and sat inside a standing result for months.

fires on 2.7% of sub-$20 bars, 100% above $333

The scoring rule can be the edge

Taking max(long, short) and comparing it to long-only is biased by construction — the max of two noisy numbers beats one of them. A null on the whole procedure catches this; a null on the model does not.

+0.33 bps from shuffled labels alone

An arbitrary seed is a free parameter

If a result moves when you reseed, that movement belongs in the error bar.

sd 0.248 against a claimed effect of 1.25

Overlapping windows and pooled symbols

Overlap inflates t by √horizon — one walked 1.60 → 5.55 → 10.78 on no new information. Pooling across symbols mostly measures which ticker it is; a market-wide day is one event, not 549.

within-symbol collapsed an R² from 0.829 to 0.329 · date clustering roughly doubled errors

Survivorship is larger than most effects being tested

The archive is today's universe; everything delisted is missing. It biases toward fake mean reversion — exactly the sign of "touching a high predicts lower returns".

15–21 percentage points a year

Report gross and cost separately

Folding a 3.10 bps charge into a headline buried a real signal for most of a session. And benchmark against the right thing: the random walk for a forecast, HAR for volatility, buy-and-hold for a strategy.

a +1.01 bps signal hidden under its own fee

Data

Two archive facts that invalidate whole question classes

Prices are split- and dividend-adjusted, and the adjustment is not invertible. The ingest fetches raw intraday from Webull, multiplies it into adjusted units, and stores only the result — the factors are never persisted. On 12 June 2015 TQQQ traded at $110.91; the archive records $2.197, a factor of 50.48. The inverse funds are worse: SOXS's adjusted 2015 price is $11 million, its maximum $8.6 billion.

This rules out the entire microstructure branch on current data, not just one test. The minimum tick is one cent, absolute — so roundness, spread width, tick clustering and where limit orders pile up are all meaningless once every historical price has been multiplied by an arbitrary factor. To recover it: persist the per-day factors (a few hundred KB, makes it invertible going forward) and re-fetch raw into a parallel archive. Webull serves raw intraday back to at least 2015; 2011 is unverified.

Costs exceed every gross effect ever measured here. Crossing the spread is ~1.35 bps round trip on TQQQ. No effect in the closed table above is larger. Options are far worse — 14.5% of mid on a short-dated TQQQ call, about a thousand times the stock's.

Where to look, in the order I would look

Every test so far has had the same shape: predict a price move, from public price data, at a horizon of one session or less, on the most arbitraged instruments in existence. Fourteen negative results in that cell are not fourteen pieces of evidence — they are one, repeated. Meanwhile the two structural advantages a small account genuinely has, it can hold and it never has to quote, have never been used.

  1. Re-run the premium test on a liquid chain

    "Selling premium is approximately zero" was measured on TQQQ, whose options spread 14.5% of mid. SPY front-month options are penny-wide — same premium, a tenth of the friction. The conclusion may be entirely an artifact of the vehicle.

    Cheapest test on the list: existing tooling, existing method, better data. Against it: one spread is one bet, and the 92%-concentration failure is already documented here.

  2. Harvest the variance drag instead of paying it

    A 3x daily-rebalanced fund loses roughly 19 points a year to (9−3)/2·σ². That is arithmetic, not a prediction. Short both sides of a levered pair and the ±3μ largely cancels while the σ² accrues. The repo already measures the decay — QQQ compounds 16.7× over 27 years, the 3x version 2.16× — but nothing harvests it.

    Best mechanism left, and it requires being right about nothing. Against it: short gamma, so a sustained trend hurts before decay accrues; inverse-ETF borrow is expensive and eats some by construction; not executable at $500.

  3. Lengthen the horizon

    At 30 minutes the competition owns colocation racks. At 6–12 months it is mandates and career risk. Nothing here has tested holding.

    Against it: slow to validate, and the archive's survivorship bias grows with horizon.

  4. Measure what a resting order actually keeps

    Whether a passive fill retains its half spread after adverse selection sets the floor for any intraday idea. A clean negative closes the whole branch, which is a real result. Needs live quotes and side-flagged ticks; the minute-bar version is settled as unworkable.

    Not a strategy — a bar every strategy has to clear. Against it: a positive answer is only ever an upper bound, because a real order sits in a queue.
